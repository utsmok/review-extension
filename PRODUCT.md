# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Academic librarians and information services staff at the University of Twente. They evaluate AI-powered academic search tools (Semantic Scholar, Elicit, Consensus, etc.) for institutional recommendation. Their context: a focused work session at a desk, often with the search tool open in the main browser tab while the review extension runs in the Chrome side panel. They need to capture evidence, score systematically, and produce an auditable report.

## Product Purpose

A browser extension that provides a structured evaluation workflow for academic search tools against the TRUST framework (Transparent, Reliable, User-centric, Secure, Traceable). The tool captures screenshots and DOM snapshots as evidence, links them to specific rubric criteria, collects scores, and exports a complete review package (ZIP with CSVs, evidence files, and a styled PDF report). All data stays local.

Success: every review is consistent, evidence-backed, and auditable by a second reviewer without context.

## Brand Personality

Authoritative, systematic, institutional. The tool should feel like a proper evaluation instrument that a university would trust, not a casual consumer app. It carries the University of Twente identity (navy primary, formal typography) but remains focused on the task, not on decoration.

## Anti-references

No strong anti-references. The University of Twente institutional brand provides sufficient visual direction.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** The tool evaluates transparency and traceability in AI tools, so it must itself make every scoring decision traceable to captured evidence. No orphan scores.

2. **Evidence over opinion.** Every score should be grounded in a screenshot or DOM capture, not subjective memory. The workflow enforces this: capture first, score against what you captured.

3. **Institutional confidence.** The design should feel like an instrument a university would issue, not a side project. Dense, flat, sharp-edged. Every element signals deliberate structure, not decorative flair.

4. **Dense, not busy.** A full TRUST rubric (quality gates + scoring criteria) must fit in a browser side panel without scrolling fatigue. Information density is a feature, but every pixel must earn its place.

5. **Structured transparency.** The visual encoding (TRUST principle accent colors, score-level colors) should make the state of a review scannable at a glance. A reviewer should see which principles are scored, which are pending, and where evidence gaps exist without reading every field.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best effort baseline: semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, reduced-motion support, focus-visible indicators. No formal WCAG compliance target yet, but the tool is intended for university-wide use and should work for all staff.
