  • As generative AI reshapes the information landscape, academic libraries face a "crisis of abundance." New AI-driven search tools—ranging from Semantic Scholar to Consensus and Elicit—are launching weekly, often blurring the line between rigorous bibliographic retrieval and non-deterministic synthesis. While libraries strive to support innovation, we must also safeguard scholarly standards of reliability and transparency. How can libraries act as effective intermediaries in this volatile environment without becoming bottlenecks? We want to develop a practical governance and evaluation framework to systematically assess AI-based search systems. Moving beyond general AI guidelines, we want to introduce a specific distinction between "deterministic" tools (e.g., standard vector search) and "generative" tools (e.g., LLM-based synthesis), applying the TRUST principles: Transparency: Does the tool disclose its methodology and data sources? Reliability: Are outputs consistent, verifiable, and free from hallucinations? User-centricity: Is the tool fit for academic workflows and accessible? Security: Does it comply with the EU AI Act, GDPR, and privacy standards? Traceability: Can every generated claim be traced back to a primary source? As far as we know, there are currently no frameworks or guidelines specifically for this topic: evaluation of search tools; AI or not.
